Choosing the Right Printer

The cheapest printer to purchase is not necessarily the cheapest printer to own. Replacement ink, toner, maintenance requirements and subscription costs can make a significant difference to the overall cost of printing.

Choosing the right printer depends on how frequently you print, what you print and which features you need.

Inkjet Printers

Inkjet printers are popular for general home use, offering colour printing, photographs and everyday document printing. They are often relatively inexpensive to purchase, although replacement ink cartridges can be costly and printheads may become blocked if the printer is used infrequently.

Laser Printers

Laser printers are particularly well suited to document printing and small office environments. They generally offer fast printing speeds, sharp text and good reliability. Toner does not dry out in the same way as liquid ink, making laser printers worth considering for occasional document printing as well.

Ink Tank Printers

Ink tank printers use refillable ink reservoirs instead of conventional cartridges. Although they typically cost more to purchase, they can offer significantly lower running costs for households and businesses that print regularly.

I provide independent advice based on your printing requirements and budget, taking into account both the initial purchase price and ongoing running costs.
