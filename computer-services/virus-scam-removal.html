Concerned About Your Computer's Security?

Computer viruses, malicious software and online scams are becoming increasingly sophisticated. Criminals often use convincing emails, websites, telephone calls and security warnings to trick people into installing software, revealing personal information or making payments.

It can be difficult to distinguish a genuine security warning from a fraudulent one, particularly when messages appear urgent or claim that your computer is already infected.

Dunmow Computer Services provides local assistance in Great Dunmow and surrounding areas, helping home users and small businesses investigate security concerns, remove malicious software and restore their computers to a safer working condition.

Think You Have Been Scammed?

If you suspect someone has gained unauthorised access to your computer, or you have been persuaded to install suspicious software, there are some important steps you can take immediately.

  • Disconnect the affected computer from the internet if you suspect someone has remote access to it.
  • Do not make payments or provide further personal information to the suspected scammer.
  • Do not call telephone numbers displayed in suspicious security warnings or pop-up messages.
  • Contact your bank immediately using a trusted telephone number if you have shared financial information or made a suspicious payment.
  • Use a separate, trusted device to change potentially compromised passwords, starting with your email account.
  • Retain suspicious emails, messages and transaction details where possible, as these may be useful evidence.
  • Arrange for your computer to be investigated before using it again for sensitive activities.

Online scams are deliberately designed to appear convincing. I provide confidential, non-judgemental assistance, focusing on understanding what has happened and helping you take appropriate steps to protect yourself.

Common Signs of Viruses & Malware

Malicious software does not always make its presence obvious. Some infections cause noticeable problems, while others operate quietly in the background.

Potential warning signs include:

  • Unexpected security warnings and persistent pop-ups.
  • Unfamiliar applications appearing on your computer.
  • Unexpected changes to your browser homepage or search engine.
  • Being redirected to unfamiliar or suspicious websites.
  • Browser extensions you do not recognise.
  • Unexplained reductions in computer performance.
  • Antivirus software becoming disabled unexpectedly.
  • Applications opening or behaving unexpectedly.
  • Unfamiliar remote access software.
  • Suspicious activity on your email or online accounts.

These symptoms do not necessarily mean your computer has been infected. Some can also be caused by legitimate software, configuration problems or hardware faults. Proper investigation is important before deciding what action is required.

Fake Antivirus Warnings & Security Pop-Ups

A common online scam involves displaying messages claiming that your computer has been infected, your antivirus subscription has expired or your personal information is at risk.

These messages often imitate well-known security companies such as Norton, McAfee, Microsoft or Bitdefender. They may use familiar logos, alarming colours and urgent language to persuade you to make a payment or contact a fraudulent support service.

Some warnings are generated by malicious websites or browser notifications rather than by installed antivirus software. Others may be associated with unwanted or malicious applications.

I can help with:

  • Persistent fake antivirus renewal messages.
  • Fraudulent virus infection warnings.
  • Unwanted browser notifications.
  • Suspicious security applications.
  • Pop-ups that are difficult to close.
  • Unwanted browser extensions and redirects.

I'll investigate where the messages are coming from, remove unwanted software or permissions where appropriate, and help you distinguish genuine security notifications from fraudulent ones.

Browser Hijacking & Unwanted Extensions

Web browsers are a common target for unwanted software. Malicious or intrusive browser extensions can change your search engine, redirect you to advertising websites, monitor browsing activity or display unwanted messages.

These changes can sometimes be difficult to reverse, particularly when unwanted software has modified browser settings or installed additional background components.

Browser security assistance can include:

  • Investigating suspicious browser extensions.
  • Restoring your preferred search engine and homepage.
  • Removing unwanted website notification permissions.
  • Investigating browser redirects and intrusive advertising.
  • Reviewing browser security and privacy settings.
  • Resetting compromised browser configurations.
  • Checking browser synchronisation settings for unwanted changes.

Where possible, I'll preserve your existing bookmarks, favourites and other important browser information while addressing the underlying problem.

Telephone Scams & Remote Access

Criminals frequently impersonate trusted organisations, including banks, broadband providers, government departments and technology companies.

They may claim that your computer has been hacked, your broadband connection is unsafe or your bank account is under threat. They may then persuade you to install remote access software so they can control your computer.

Remote access applications have legitimate uses, but when installed at the request of a scammer they can provide access to sensitive information, online accounts and personal files.

If you have allowed someone suspicious to access your computer, I can help by:

  • Investigating installed remote access applications.
  • Removing unwanted remote access software.
  • Checking for additional suspicious applications.
  • Reviewing relevant system and security settings.
  • Assessing whether further security measures are required.
  • Advising on appropriate account protection measures.

Removing remote access software alone may not be sufficient following a serious security incident. Depending on what access was provided, a complete operating system reinstallation may be the safer option.

Email Scams & Compromised Accounts

Fraudulent emails and messages often attempt to persuade recipients to open malicious attachments, visit fake websites or enter their account passwords.

A compromised email account can be particularly serious because email is frequently used to reset passwords for other online services.

Account security assistance can include:

  • Investigating suspicious email account activity.
  • Assisting with password changes and account recovery.
  • Reviewing account recovery details.
  • Checking for unfamiliar email forwarding rules.
  • Reviewing connected devices and active sessions.
  • Enabling two-factor authentication.
  • Advising on secure password management.

Account recovery depends on the security procedures of the relevant service provider. While I can assist with the recovery process, access to compromised accounts cannot always be restored.

Ransomware & Inaccessible Files

Ransomware is a particularly damaging form of malicious software that can encrypt personal or business files, preventing you from accessing them unless a ransom is paid.

If your files have suddenly become inaccessible or you have received a ransom demand, disconnect the affected computer from your network and avoid connecting external backup drives until the situation has been assessed.

I can help assess the situation, investigate available backups and advise on appropriate recovery options. More serious ransomware incidents may require specialist cybersecurity or data recovery services.

Recovery of encrypted or damaged files cannot be guaranteed, and paying a ransom does not guarantee that your files will be restored.

Recognising Common Online Scams

Understanding how online scams work can help you recognise suspicious activity before it becomes a problem.

Common warning signs include:

  • Unexpected calls claiming your computer has been infected.
  • Requests to install remote access software.
  • Messages demanding immediate payment or action.
  • Requests for passwords or one-time security codes.
  • Unexpected antivirus subscription renewal notices.
  • Requests to transfer money to a supposedly safe account.
  • Unsolicited emails containing unexpected links or attachments.
  • Websites offering alarming or unexpected security warnings.

If you are unsure whether a message or telephone call is genuine, contact the organisation directly using a telephone number or website you already know and trust, rather than the contact details provided in the suspicious message.

Further guidance on staying safe online is available from the National Cyber Security Centre .
