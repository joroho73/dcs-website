Cloud Storage Is Not Always a Backup

Many people assume that because their files are stored in OneDrive, Google Drive or another cloud storage service, they are automatically protected against every type of data loss.

However, file synchronisation is designed to keep files consistent across multiple devices. If a file is accidentally deleted, overwritten or damaged, those changes may also be synchronised to the cloud.

Some cloud storage services provide deleted file recovery and previous file versions, but these features may have limitations or retention periods.

A separate backup provides an additional layer of protection, allowing you to recover important files even when your original files and synchronised copies have been affected.

The 3-2-1 Backup Principle

A widely recommended approach to protecting important information is known as the 3-2-1 backup principle.

  • 3 copies: Keep your original files and at least two backup copies.
  • 2 types of storage: Store your information on at least two different types of storage media.
  • 1 off-site copy: Keep at least one backup in a separate physical location.

For example, you might keep your photographs and documents on your computer, maintain a backup on an external hard drive and keep an additional backup using a dedicated online backup service.

This provides protection against a wider range of problems, including hardware failure, accidental deletion, theft and damage to your property.

Data Recovery & Inaccessible Files

If your computer has stopped working, your external hard drive is no longer accessible or important files appear to have disappeared, it may still be possible to retrieve some or all of your data.

  • Investigating inaccessible files and folders.
  • Resolving file ownership and permission issues.
  • Restoring files from existing backups.
  • Investigating accidentally deleted files.
  • Recovering available previous file versions.

If you suspect a storage drive is failing or important files have been accidentally deleted, avoid continuing to use the affected drive. Installing software or saving additional files may reduce the chances of successful recovery.

Data recovery is not always possible. Physically damaged drives, severely corrupted storage devices and some encrypted data may require specialist recovery services.

I'll assess the situation and explain the available options without making unrealistic promises about the likelihood of recovering your files.
